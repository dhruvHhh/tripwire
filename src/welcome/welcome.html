Tripwire is installed

It checks the links on the pages you visit and marks the risky ones before you click.

What it does

On every page, Tripwire looks at each link: where it really goes, whether that address imitates a well-known site, whether the link's text says one site while the link goes to another, and whether the address is on a blocklist of known phishing and malware pages. Links it has doubts about get a small coloured dot at their top right corner. Rest the pointer on a dot to see the reasons and the real destination.

If you click a red link, Tripwire asks first. It shows where the link really goes and why it was flagged, and you choose "Go back" or "Continue anyway". You can turn that off on the options page.

It starts working on pages you open from now on. Tabs that were already open need a reload first.

What the colours mean

  • Red: likely dangerous The address is on a blocklist, or it imitates a well-known site. Don't sign in or download anything there unless you are sure.
  • Amber: suspicious Something about the link is unusual, such as a shortened address or a raw IP address. Check where it goes before you follow it.
  • Green: no red flags found Tripwire found nothing wrong. That is not a guarantee. Green dots are hidden unless you ask for them, to keep pages quiet.

The number on the Tripwire toolbar icon is how many risky links the page has: red if any are dangerous, amber if they are only suspicious.

The privacy promise

The links you see never leave your browser.

Every check runs on your device. The blocklists are downloaded to your browser and looked up there; Tripwire never sends a page's address or its links to anyone, and it has no account, no analytics and no ads.

Choosing what you see

Click the Tripwire icon in the toolbar to open its popup. There you can pick how badges are shown, for one site or for all of them:

  • Risky only (the usual setting)A badge on suspicious and dangerous links only.
  • Show allA badge on every link, including the ones with no red flags.
  • Only when I clickNo badges until you ask for them in the popup. The toolbar icon still counts risky links.
  • Off for this siteNothing is checked on that site.

The popup also lists the page's flagged links with every reason, and lets you trust a domain you know so it stops being flagged. Trusted domains and site settings can be reviewed on the options page.

If you can't see the icon, open the extensions menu (the puzzle piece in the toolbar) and pin Tripwire.

Tripwire is a second opinion, not a guarantee. A link with no badge can still be harmful, and a red one can be a false alarm. When something matters, check the address yourself.
